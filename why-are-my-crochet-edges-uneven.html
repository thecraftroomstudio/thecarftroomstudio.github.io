Why Are My Crochet Edges Uneven?

7 common causes of uneven crochet edges — and simple ways to fix them.

You're following the pattern, your stitches look fine, but somehow your crochet project keeps getting wider, narrower, or wavy along the edges.

Don't worry. Uneven crochet edges are one of the most common problems beginners experience.

In most cases, the problem comes down to where you place your hook, how you handle the turning chain, or accidentally gaining or losing stitches.

Why do my crochet edges look uneven?

Here are seven common reasons your crochet edges may not be straight:

  1. You're missing the first or last stitch.
  2. You're accidentally adding an extra stitch.
  3. Your turning chain is confusing you.
  4. You're placing your hook in the wrong part of a stitch.
  5. Your tension changes while you crochet.
  6. Your stitch count is changing from row to row.
  7. You are working the turning chain differently from the pattern.

1. You're missing the first or last stitch

This is one of the biggest causes of uneven crochet edges.

At the beginning or end of a row, it can be difficult to see exactly where the first or last stitch is. If you accidentally skip one, your project becomes narrower.

If you repeatedly miss stitches, your edges may gradually slope inward.

Try this:

Before moving to the next row, count your stitches. Compare that number with the number required by your pattern.

You can also place a stitch marker in the first or last stitch to make the edges easier to identify.

2. You're accidentally adding an extra stitch

Uneven edges can also happen when you accidentally crochet into a place that isn't supposed to receive a stitch.

This can happen near the beginning or end of a row, especially when the turning chain looks like a normal stitch.

Try this:

Count your stitches after every row while you're learning. If the number keeps increasing, look closely at the beginning and end of your rows.

3. Your turning chain is causing confusion

Turning chains can make the edge of a project look confusing because their role depends on the stitch and pattern you're using.

Some patterns count the turning chain as a stitch, while others don't. Always follow the instructions given by your pattern.

Beginner tip:

Don't automatically treat every turning chain as a stitch. Check the pattern instructions first.

4. You're putting your hook in the wrong place

Crochet stitches have specific places where the hook should be inserted. If you accidentally work into a gap, loop, or nearby stitch, your stitch count and edge can change.

This becomes especially noticeable along the first and last stitches of a row.

If you're still learning how stitches are formed, review our guide to basic crochet stitches.

5. Your tension keeps changing

Your stitches may technically be correct, but your edges can still look uneven if your tension changes throughout the project.

For example, you might crochet tightly when you start a row and more loosely as you become comfortable.

Try this:

Relax your hands and avoid pulling the yarn too tightly. Try to keep your movements and yarn tension as consistent as possible.

6. Your stitch count is changing

If one row has a different number of stitches from the previous row, your edges will eventually stop lining up.

Stitch counting may feel slow at first, but it can save you from having to unravel several rows later.

Learn more in our guide: How to Count Crochet Stitches.

7. You're treating every pattern the same way

Not every crochet pattern handles turning chains, first stitches, and last stitches in exactly the same way.

One pattern may tell you to crochet into the first stitch. Another may use the turning chain as part of the stitch count.

That's why it's important to follow the specific instructions for the pattern you're making rather than relying on one universal rule.

A simple way to fix uneven crochet edges

If your project is already becoming uneven, stop and check your stitch count before continuing.

  1. Count the stitches in your current row.
  2. Compare the number with the pattern.
  3. Check the first stitch.
  4. Check the last stitch.
  5. Look for accidental extra stitches.
  6. Check whether the turning chain counts as a stitch.
  7. Continue only after you understand where the stitch count changed.

Beginner checklist for straight crochet edges

  • ☐ Count your stitches regularly.
  • ☐ Find your first stitch before starting the row.
  • ☐ Find your last stitch before turning.
  • ☐ Follow the pattern's turning-chain instructions.
  • ☐ Keep your tension reasonably consistent.
  • ☐ Use stitch markers when you're unsure.
  • ☐ Don't rush the end of each row.
