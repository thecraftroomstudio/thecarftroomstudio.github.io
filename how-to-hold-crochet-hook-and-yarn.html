BEGINNER GUIDE

Your crochet grip doesn't have to be perfect.

When you're learning crochet, it can feel strange to hold the hook and yarn at the same time. Your hands may feel stiff, your stitches may become too tight, or the yarn may keep slipping.

The good news is that there isn't one perfect way to hold a crochet hook. The goal is to find a comfortable position that gives you enough control without squeezing the hook or yarn too tightly.

STEP 01

How to hold the crochet hook

Most beginners use one of two common grips: the pencil grip or the knife grip.

PENCIL GRIP

Hold it like a pencil

Place your thumb and index finger around the middle of the hook, similar to how you would hold a pencil.

Your other fingers can rest underneath the hook for support. Keep your hand relaxed rather than gripping tightly.

KNIFE GRIP

Hold it like a knife

Place your hand over the hook and hold it between your thumb and fingers.

This grip can feel more natural for people who prefer to move the whole hand rather than mainly the fingers.

STEP 02

How to hold the yarn

The hand holding the yarn controls how easily the yarn feeds toward your hook.

For many beginners, the yarn is wrapped loosely around the fingers of the non-hook hand. The exact arrangement can vary depending on what feels comfortable.

01

Let the yarn come from the ball

Keep the yarn attached to the ball or skein and allow it to feed toward your working hand.

02

Guide the yarn with your fingers

Use your fingers to gently guide the yarn toward the hook.

03

Keep your hand relaxed

You don't need to squeeze the yarn. It should be able to slide through your fingers with gentle resistance.

STEP 03

How to control crochet tension

Tension simply means how tightly or loosely you hold and work the yarn.

Beginners often pull the yarn too tightly because they are trying to keep every stitch under control. This can make it difficult to insert the hook and can make your hands tired.

Try this instead

Let the yarn move naturally through your fingers. Your stitches should have enough room for the hook to move through them without forcing it.

QUICK CHECK

Is your grip too tight?

You may be holding your yarn or hook too tightly if:

The hook is difficult to move

If you have to force the hook through almost every stitch, loosen your grip slightly.

Your stitches are very small

Very tight stitches can make it difficult to see where the next stitch belongs.

Your hand gets tired quickly

Frequent breaks and a more relaxed grip can make learning much more comfortable.

BEGINNER PRACTICE

Practice before starting a project.

Don't worry about making something beautiful immediately.

Make a short chain, undo it, and make another one. Focus on keeping your hook and yarn relaxed rather than trying to make every stitch perfect.

After a few minutes, your hands will begin to understand the movement more naturally.

COMMON BEGINNER MISTAKES

Avoid these three habits.

01

Squeezing the hook

A tight grip can make your hand tired and restrict the movement of the hook.

02

Pulling the yarn constantly

The yarn needs to flow. Constantly pulling it tight can make your stitches smaller and harder to work.

03

Copying someone else's grip exactly

Your hands are different from everyone else's. Use other crocheters as a guide, then adjust the grip until it feels comfortable for you.

BEGINNER CHECKLIST

Before you start crocheting

✓ Your hook feels comfortable in your hand.

✓ Your yarn can move freely.

✓ Your fingers aren't squeezing tightly.

✓ Your shoulders and hands feel relaxed.

✓ You can move the hook without forcing it.
