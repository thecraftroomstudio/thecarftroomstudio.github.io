Why Is My Crochet Getting Wider?

If your crochet project keeps getting wider as you work, don't panic. You are probably adding stitches somewhere without realizing it. Let's find the problem and fix it.

COMMON REASONS

Why is my crochet getting wider?

If each row seems longer than the one before it, you're probably adding stitches somewhere. These are some of the first things to check.

01

You're adding an extra stitch

An extra stitch can easily be added near the beginning or end of a row. Count your stitches after every row while you're learning.

02

You're working into the turning chain

Depending on the pattern, the turning chain may or may not count as a stitch. Follow the pattern instructions carefully so you don't accidentally add an extra stitch.

03

You're working into the wrong place

Make sure your hook is going into the correct stitch instead of accidentally working into a gap or space beside it.

04

Your stitch count is changing

Write down the number of stitches you should have in each row. Then compare your actual count before starting the next row.

QUICK CHECK

Let's find where the extra stitches are coming from.

Start by checking the beginning and end of your last few rows. These are common places for beginners to accidentally add stitches.

Check the beginning → Check the ending → Check your stitch count → Check your tension →

CHECK 01

Check the beginning of the row

The first few stitches can be easy to miss or accidentally work into twice. Look closely at the first stitch of your previous row and where you placed your hook after making the turning chain.

If you are unsure where the first stitch is, place a stitch marker in it. This can make the edge much easier to recognize as you continue working.

CHECK 02

Check the end of the row

The last stitch is another common trouble spot. It can be easy to accidentally work into a nearby space or add an extra stitch at the edge.

Before turning your work, count the stitches in the row. If the number is higher than the pattern says it should be, you have probably added a stitch somewhere.

CHECK 03

Count your stitches after every row

When you're learning crochet, counting your stitches may feel slow, but it can save you from having to undo several rows later.

For example, if your pattern says you should have 20 stitches, check that you have exactly 20 before beginning the next row.

CHECK 04

Check your tension

Tension can change the size and appearance of your crochet. If your stitches become very loose, your fabric may look larger, but a project that keeps getting wider from row to row usually means your stitch count needs checking first.

Focus on making each stitch in the same general way rather than trying to pull the yarn extremely tight.

A SIMPLE FIX

What should you do if your crochet is already wider?

First, stop and count the stitches in your current row. Compare that number with the number your pattern expects.

If you have extra stitches, look back over the last few rows and check the beginning and ending stitches. Finding the first row where your stitch count changed will usually help you locate where the extra stitch was added.

Once you find the mistake, you can undo the affected stitches and work the row again. It is much easier to fix the problem now than after several more rows.

BEGINNER CHECKLIST

Before you continue crocheting, ask yourself:

Did I find the first stitch? ✓ Did I work into the correct last stitch? ✓ Does my stitch count match the pattern? ✓ Is my tension reasonably consistent? ✓
