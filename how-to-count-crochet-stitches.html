STITCHES & COUNTING

How to Count Crochet Stitches and Rows

If your crochet project keeps changing size, counting your stitches can help you find the problem. Here's a simple way for beginners to count stitches and rows.

STEP BY STEP

How to count crochet stitches

The easiest way to learn is to count one stitch at a time instead of trying to recognise the whole row at once.

01

Find the top of the row

Look along the top edge of your crochet. Each completed stitch usually forms a small V shape. These V shapes are what you'll count.

02

Count each V

Starting from one side, count each visible V as you move across the row.

03

Check your first stitch

Beginners sometimes skip the first stitch or accidentally count the turning chain as a stitch. Follow your pattern carefully.

04

Count every row

When learning, count your stitches at the end of every row. This makes mistakes much easier to catch early.

COMMON PROBLEM

Why do I keep losing stitches?

If the number of stitches gets smaller from one row to the next, you may be accidentally skipping a stitch near the beginning or end of the row.

Check the first stitch → Check the last stitch → Check the turning chain → Count before starting the next row →

CHECK 01

How to find the first stitch.

The first stitch can be easy to overlook, especially when you're turning your work. Take a moment to identify exactly where your pattern tells you to place the first stitch.

CHECK 02

How to find the last stitch.

The last stitch can sometimes hide along the edge. Before turning your work, check the entire row from beginning to end.

CHECK 03

Understand the turning chain.

Whether a turning chain counts as a stitch depends on the pattern and stitch being used. Always follow the instructions for your specific pattern.

CHECK 04

Count before moving on.

Once you finish a row, count your stitches before beginning the next one. If the number isn't correct, it is usually easier to find the mistake now than several rows later.
