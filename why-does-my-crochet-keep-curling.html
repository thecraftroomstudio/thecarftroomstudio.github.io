Why Does My Crochet Keep Curling?

If your crochet project refuses to lie flat and keeps curling upward, don't worry. Curling is a common beginner problem, and the cause is often easier to fix than it looks.

COMMON REASONS

Why does crochet curl?

Crochet can curl for several reasons. Some are related to tension, while others come from the stitch or pattern you're using. Start by checking these common causes.

01

Your tension is too tight

Pulling the yarn very tightly can make the fabric stiff and cause the edges to curl. Try relaxing your grip and allowing the yarn to move more naturally through each stitch.

02

Your stitches are uneven

If some stitches are much tighter or looser than others, your fabric may not sit flat. Try to make your stitches with a similar amount of tension.

03

The stitch naturally curls

Some crochet stitches and patterns naturally create a curved or textured fabric. Not every curl means you have made a mistake.

04

Your project needs more rows

Some crochet fabrics curl during the early rows and become flatter as the project grows. Keep following the pattern before deciding that something is wrong.

QUICK CHECK

Before you undo your work, check these four things.

A little curling does not always mean you need to start again. First check your tension, stitch consistency, pattern instructions, and the number of rows you've completed.

CHECK 01

Relax your crochet tension

One of the most common reasons beginner crochet curls is overly tight tension. If you are gripping the hook tightly or pulling the working yarn after every stitch, your fabric can become stiff.

Try holding the hook comfortably and letting the yarn slide through your fingers. Your stitches should be secure, but they should not feel squeezed onto the hook.

CHECK 02

Look at your stitches

Take a close look at several stitches in the same row. Are they approximately the same size? If some are very tight and others are very loose, the fabric may curl or become uneven.

You do not need perfectly identical stitches. The goal is simply to develop a comfortable, consistent rhythm.

CHECK 03

Check the pattern instructions

Some patterns are designed to curl, fold, or form a curved shape. If the pattern is meant to create a hat, flower, basket, or another shaped project, curling may be part of the design.

Before changing your technique, check what the finished project is supposed to look like.

CHECK 04

Check whether you've worked enough rows

If you have only completed a few rows, give your project a little more time. Some crochet fabric looks curled or uneven at the beginning and becomes easier to handle as more rows are added.

If the project is supposed to be a flat square or rectangle and the curling continues, go back through the earlier rows and check your tension and stitch count.

HOW TO FIX IT

What can you do if your crochet is already curling?

First, identify the cause instead of immediately pulling everything apart. If your tension is too tight, try loosening it on the next few rows and compare the result.

If the problem comes from a mistake in the pattern or stitch placement, find the row where the problem started and correct it there.

For a finished piece that is supposed to lie flat, blocking may also help depending on the yarn and project. Always follow the care instructions for your yarn before applying heat or moisture.

BEGINNER CHECKLIST

Before starting again, ask yourself:

Am I pulling the yarn too tightly? ✓ Are my stitches reasonably consistent? ✓ Is the pattern supposed to lie flat? ✓ Have I completed enough rows? ✓
